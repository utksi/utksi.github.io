    Relaxed-ion constants¶

    When a crystal is strained, atoms that are not fixed by symmetry move inside the cell. The constants with the atoms held at their strained fractional positions are the clamped-ion constants \(C^0\). Experiments measure the relaxed-ion constants, because at acoustic frequencies the atoms follow the strain:

    \[ C = C^0 - \frac{1}{\Omega}\, \Lambda^{\mathsf T} \Phi^{+} \Lambda , \]

    lowered by the relaxation of the atoms. Here \(\Lambda\) is the internal-strain tensor (force on each atom per unit strain), \(\Phi\) the \(\Gamma\)-point force constants and \(\Omega\) the cell volume (theory). If every atom sits at a centre of inversion, as in fcc, bcc, rocksalt or cubic perovskites, nothing relaxes and \(C = C^0\).

    minelastic computes \(C\) in two ways:

    relax the ions displace the atoms
    option --relax-ions, relax_ions=True --ionic, ionic=True
    calculations one relaxation per strained cell one single point per strained cell, plus one per symmetry-irreducible atomic displacement and step
    also gives the force constants and, with dipoles, the Born charges and piezoelectric tensors
    watch out for relaxations must be converged tightly: a strain of 0.01 induces forces of only about 0.05 eV/Å an unstable or soft optical mode makes the relaxed-ion response undefined; minelastic stops with an error

    The second route is the linear response itself, the method of VASP's IBRION = 6. It needs no relaxations, which makes it cheaper and more robust in most cases. The two routes differ by terms of second order in the strain step.

    With an ASE calculator¶

    examples/emt_ionic.py relaxes hcp Cu with EMT and computes the relaxed-ion constants by both routes, and by relaxing the ions in the six single strains:

    relaxed = minelastic.calculate(atoms, EMT(), relax_ions=True, fmax=1e-6)          # route 1
ionic = minelastic.calculate(atoms, EMT(), ionic=True)                             # route 2
six = minelastic.calculate(atoms, EMT(), relax_ions=True, fmax=1e-6, scheme="asess")

    reference: a = 2.5386 A, c = 4.1430 A

        clamped C0  relaxed (1)  ionic (2)   six (3)   GPa
c11         233.60       216.34     216.34    216.35
c12          94.81       112.07     112.07    112.07
c13          74.77        74.77      74.77     74.78
c33         254.00       254.00     254.00    254.00
c44          49.30        49.30      49.30     49.30

(1) 8 relaxations; (2) 8 strained and 8 displaced-atom single points; (3) 24 relaxations
largest difference (2) - (1): 0.003 GPa

Gamma-point force constants of atom 1 on itself (eV/A^2):
[[ 1.6453  0.     -0.    ]
 [ 0.      1.6453 -0.    ]
 [-0.     -0.      8.4942]]

    The routes agree to 0.003 GPa. hcp has no optical mode that couples linearly to the yz and xz shears, so c44 is the same with clamped and relaxed ions.

    With VASP¶

    minelastic generate vasp vasp_aln -o el_relaxed --relax-ions --stencil central     # route 1
minelastic generate vasp vasp_aln -o el_ionic --ionic --displacement 0.015 --stencil central   # route 2

    With --relax-ions, generate sets IBRION = 2, NSW = 99, ISIF = 2 and, unless the template sets it, EDIFFG = -5E-4. With --ionic, wurtzite AlN needs its two inequivalent atoms displaced along x and z: with two points per displacement, 8 displaced cells in addition to the 4 strained cells. VASP's own IBRION = 6 uses the same 8 displaced cells but 12 strained cells, because it always applies the six single strains. For the template of the VASP tutorial, with two points per strain:

    GPa c11 c12 c13 c33 c44 c66
    clamped ions \(C^0\) (4 cells) 440.26 96.78 62.16 432.32 125.02 171.74
    relaxed ions, route 1 (4 relaxations) 375.66 127.98 97.63 355.22 112.26 123.84
    relaxed ions, route 2 (4 + 8 single points) 376.11 128.41 97.71 354.60 112.47 123.85
    VASP IBRION = 6, ISIF = 3 (12 + 8 single points) 376.33 128.55 97.79 355.24 112.57 123.89

    The differences of a few tenths of a GPa come from the numerical derivatives: VASP strains the cell by 0.015 and differentiates \(J\sigma\), minelastic used 0.01 and the stress itself. The run of route 2 also gives the Born charges and the piezoelectric tensors (Born charges and piezoelectricity).

    With Quantum ESPRESSO¶

    minelastic generate qe qe_aln/pw.in -o el_relaxed --relax-ions --stencil central

    sets calculation = 'relax' with forc_conv_thr = 2.0d-5 and etot_conv_thr = 1.0d-8 unless the template sets them; the pw.x defaults stop the relaxation before the internal strain is relaxed. For the template of the Quantum ESPRESSO tutorial:

    GPa c11 c12 c13 c33 c44
    minimal set (4 relaxations) 377.67 132.00 101.72 356.99 111.51
    six single strains (12 relaxations) 377.62 132.09 101.70 356.99 111.45

    Route 2 with pw.x (--ionic, 12 single points) agrees with the relaxations to 1.1 GPa.

    With other codes¶

    Both routes work with every interface. With ABINIT, --relax-ions appends ionmov 2, ntime 100 and tolmxf 1.0d-6 unless the input sets ionmov; with FHI-aims, it adds relax_geometry bfgs 1E-3. With CP2K through ASE, ionic=True lowers c44 of diamond Si from the clamped-ion 102.6 GPa to 79.7 GPa (CP2K tutorial).
