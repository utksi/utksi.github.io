    The method¶

    Here we explain the method of the paper (arXiv:2610.11733) and give the formulas for every quantity that minelastic computes. None of this is needed to use minelastic; the tutorials and how-to guides can be followed without it.

    We first show why a few strained cells suffice, how the strain patterns are chosen and why no stress-strain scheme can use fewer. Definitions and formulas for each quantity are on separate pages: elastic constants, relaxed-ion constants, internal-strain and force-constant tensors, piezoelectric tensors, finite-temperature constants and cost.

    Stress-strain finite differences¶

    A homogeneous strain \(\varepsilon\) (Voigt vector with engineering shear components) is applied to the lattice vectors of the relaxed reference cell, and the stress of the strained cell is computed. To first order, \(\sigma_i = \sum_j C_{ij}\,\varepsilon_j\). A strain pattern is a direction \(u\) in strain space, applied at \(\pm\delta\). The central difference

    \[ \frac{\sigma(+\delta u) - \sigma(-\delta u)}{2\delta} = C\,u + O(\delta^2) \]

    gives six linear relations between the stress components and the elastic constants. The default four-point formula, \([\sigma(-2\delta u) - 8\sigma(-\delta u) + 8\sigma(\delta u) - \sigma(2\delta u)]/12\delta\), gives the same relations with an error of order \(\delta^4\). The point group reduces \(C\) to \(n\) independent constants, from 21 (triclinic) to 3 (cubic). The question is how few patterns determine all of them.

    Symmetry-adapted form¶

    minelastic computes the form of \(C\) numerically from the Cartesian point-group rotations of the given cell. It group-averages the 21 symmetric unit matrices in Mandel notation, so the form holds in any orientation. Each independent constant is labelled by its leading matrix entry (c11, c12, ...), and dependent entries are written out, e.g. \(C_{66} = (c_{11}-c_{12})/2\) for hexagonal crystals (minelastic info).

    Uncoupled patterns and minimal sets¶

    Let the pattern \(u\) have nonzero amplitudes on the Voigt components in its support \(S\). There are \(2^6 - 1 = 63\) possible supports. The stress component \(\sigma_i\) of the pattern is uncoupled if, for generic amplitudes on \(S\), it depends on a single independent constant. That constant then follows from one stress component of one strained cell. For a cubic crystal, the single pattern \(u = (1,0,0,1,0,0)\) (xx + yz) gives

    \[ \sigma_{xx} = c_{11}\,\delta, \qquad \sigma_{yy} = \sigma_{zz} = c_{12}\,\delta, \qquad \sigma_{yz} = c_{44}\,\delta , \]

    so all three constants come from one strained cell.

    The paper constructs the patterns from the sparsity of the symmetry-reduced stiffness matrix, represented by an auxiliary binary matrix. A greedy procedure selects supports until every constant appears in an uncoupled stress component. Since there are only 63 supports, the minimum number can be verified by exhaustive search. minelastic accepts the greedy set for a given crystal only if it reaches the lower bound derived below, so it is provably minimal. Otherwise it searches all combinations of supports. The amplitude signs are chosen such that no uncoupled relation cancels. The resulting sets, for the standard settings:

    elastic type example SG constants patterns minimal set
    triclinic 2 21 6 xx ; yy ; zz ; yz ; xz ; xy
    monoclinic (unique axis b) 12 13 4 xx + yz ; yy + xy ; zz ; xz
    orthorhombic 70 9 3 xx + yz + xz + xy ; yy ; zz
    tetragonal I 88 7 2 xx + yz ; zz + xy
    tetragonal II 136 6 2 xx + yz + xy ; zz
    trigonal I 148 7 2 xx ; zz + yz
    trigonal II 154 6 2 xx ; zz + yz
    hexagonal 194 5 2 xx + yz ; zz
    cubic 225 3 1 xx + yz

    Extraction and consistency checks¶

    • uncoupled (default): each constant is the average of all stress components that contain it alone. For cubic crystals, for example, c12 is the average from \(\sigma_{yy}\) and \(\sigma_{zz}\).
    • designated: only the first such component, as in the tables of the paper.
    • lstsq: least squares over all stress components of all patterns.

    The stress components that contain several constants are not used for the extraction. They are compared with the fitted tensor and reported as checks (summary line "consistency"). A large mismatch points to nonlinearity, noise, or a reference structure that is not relaxed.

    Why fewer cells are impossible¶

    The strains invariant under the point group, i.e. the free lattice parameters, form a subspace \(V_0\) of dimension 6, 4, 3, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 1 for the nine types above. Every rotation maps the \(V_0\)-component of a strain onto itself. Suppose fewer strained cells were used than \(\dim V_0\). Then there is an invariant strain \(w \neq 0\) orthogonal to the \(V_0\)-components of all applied strains. Adding \(t\,w w^{\mathsf T}\) to \(C\) (in Mandel notation) leaves every computed stress unchanged, yet it is an admissible elastic tensor of the same symmetry. So \(C\) would be undetermined. The minimal uncoupled sets reach this bound in every class. No scheme, coupled or uncoupled, can use fewer strained cells.

    Internal-strain and piezoelectric tensors¶

    Let a space-group operation \(\{R|t\}\) map atom \(n\) onto atom \(g(n)\). Then the forces satisfy \(F_{g(n)}(R\varepsilon R^{\mathsf T}) = R\,F_n(\varepsilon)\). The forces of the few strained cells therefore give the response of every atom to the whole symmetry orbit of the applied strains. That orbit spans all six strain components, since otherwise the elastic tensor would be undetermined by the argument above. The internal-strain tensor \(\Lambda_n = \partial F_n/\partial\varepsilon\) of every atom follows exactly, by least squares over the orbit (collect --internal-strain).

    The polarization is a polar vector, \(P(R\varepsilon R^{\mathsf T}) = R\,P(\varepsilon)\), so the polarizations of the same cells determine the whole piezoelectric tensor \(e_{iJ} = \partial P_i/\partial\varepsilon_J\) in the same way. Berry-phase dipoles are defined only modulo a quantum. minelastic writes the dipole of each cell in lattice coordinates, \(p = \sum_a \varphi_a \mathbf{a}_a\), and aligns them with the reference. This gives the proper piezoelectric response, \(e = \Omega_0^{-1} A_0\, \partial\varphi/\partial\varepsilon\) (D. Vanderbilt, J. Phys. Chem. Solids 61, 147 (2000)). Clamped-ion cells give the clamped-ion tensor \(e^0\).

    Relaxed-ion response from clamped calculations¶

    With atomic displacements \(u\) and strain \(\varepsilon\), the energy per cell is, to second order,

    \[ E(u, \varepsilon) = \frac{\Omega}{2}\,\varepsilon^{\mathsf T} C^0 \varepsilon - u^{\mathsf T}\Lambda\,\varepsilon + \frac12\, u^{\mathsf T}\Phi\, u , \qquad P = e^0\varepsilon + \frac{1}{\Omega}\, Z^* u . \]

    Here \(\Phi\) are the \(\Gamma\)-point force constants, \(\Lambda\) the internal-strain tensor, and \(Z^*\) the Born effective charges. Relaxing the ions, \(u = \Phi^{+}\Lambda\varepsilon\), gives

    \[ C = C^0 - \frac{1}{\Omega}\,\Lambda^{\mathsf T}\Phi^{+}\Lambda , \qquad e = e^0 + \frac{1}{\Omega}\, Z^*\Phi^{+}\Lambda , \qquad d = e\,C^{-1} , \]

    where \(\Phi^{+}\) is the inverse of \(\Phi\) on the complement of the three uniform translations (X. Wu, D. Vanderbilt and D. R. Hamann, Phys. Rev. B 72, 035105 (2005)). With --ionic, minelastic displaces only the symmetry-irreducible atoms. Each one moves along x, then y, then z, as long as the new direction enlarges the span of its images under the site symmetry. \(\Phi\) and \(Z^*\) are completed over the symmetry orbit of the displacements. \(\Lambda\) is taken from the stresses of the displaced cells (the Maxwell relation \(\partial\sigma/\partial u = -\Lambda^{\mathsf T}/\Omega\), as in VASP). The forces in the strained cells give it a second time, as a cross-check.

    Finite temperature¶

    At finite temperature, \(C_{ij}(T) = \partial\langle\sigma_i\rangle/\partial\varepsilon_j\), with the stresses averaged over NVT trajectories in the strained cells of the thermally expanded lattice. The strain sets are the same. See Finite temperature for the protocol, the kinetic term and the error estimates.
