    Use another MLIP¶

    The released networks expect their inputs from MACE-OMAT-0 and from no other potential. Features of another potential give them inputs they were not trained on. To use another potential, write a featurizer for it, check it, and retrain the networks with the pipeline (Retrain).

    The four inputs¶

    A featurizer (recast.featurizers.Featurizer) computes any of the four inputs of the networks from a calculated structure, and each input may come from its own featurizer:

    Input Shipped networks What it is
    cell none (the atoms descriptors) per-atom descriptors of the cell networks, when they differ from atoms
    atoms MACE-OMAT-0 per-atom descriptors of the networks
    hessian MACE-OMAT-0 the Green-Lagrange Hessian H (blocks K, Lam, Phi), stored on its retained modes in float64
    relax MACE-OMAT-0 + D3(BJ) the Green-Lagrange relaxation strains T1 (with dispersion), D = T1 - T0, and their masks

    recast.featurizers.Inputs names the featurizer of each input. write_features computes them for a list of structures and records the name of each featurizer in features.json. A release records the names it was trained with, and recast correct refuses features from other models.

    A featurizer from any ASE calculator¶

    ASEFeaturizer needs only an ASE calculator and a descriptor function. The Hessian follows by central differences of the calculator's gradient in the coordinates q = (gamma(E), u) (recast.features.hessian_fd: the stress and the forces of the deformed structure, steps 1e-3 in strain and 1e-3 angstrom), and the relaxations by ASE (FixSymmetry, FrechetCellFilter, FIRE, fmax 0.01 eV/angstrom), the second one with D3(BJ) added. A potential written in PyTorch can give the exact Hessian by automatic differentiation instead, as MACEFeaturizer.hessian does.

    from recast.featurizers import ASEFeaturizer, Inputs


def my_calculator(precision):
    """The potential as an ASE calculator; precision is 'float32' (first relaxation stage) or 'float64'."""
    from my_mlip import MyCalculator

    return MyCalculator(dtype=precision)


def my_descriptors(Z, f, L):
    """Per-atom invariant descriptors (n x D) of the structure with atomic numbers Z, fractional coordinates f and
    cell rows L (angstrom), for example the scalar channels of every layer of the network."""
    ...


feat = ASEFeaturizer(my_calculator, my_descriptors, name="my-mlip")
inputs = Inputs(cell=None, atoms=feat, hessian=feat, relax=feat)

    Save a function that returns inputs in an importable module, and give it to the commands as --inputs my_module:my_inputs. A subclass of Featurizer can instead override descriptors, calculator, hessian or relaxations; MACEFeaturizer and ESENFeaturizer are two examples.

    Check it¶

    recast.contract measures what the networks rely on: descriptors invariant under rotations, reflections and translations, a Hessian that transforms with the structure, relaxation strains that rotate with it, and the blocks K, Lam and Phi of the Hessian against second differences of the energy.

    from recast import contract as C
import numpy as np

v = C.hessian_violation(feat, Z, f, L, Q=np.diag([-1.0, 1, 1]))  # a mirror
u = C.units_check(feat, Z, f, L)

    tests/test_featurizer_contract.py runs these checks in double precision. MACE-OMAT-0 passes them to rounding. eSEN-30M-OMat passes them under rotations and translations but not under reflections: its energy is not invariant under improper operations, and its Hessian changes by 0.6 % (median) and up to 4.6 % under them. Its descriptors can be averaged with the inversion image, which makes them invariant (recast.esen, used by the paper's variant of the cell estimators); its Hessian cannot be repaired that way. A new potential whose Hessian fails the reflection check will break the space group of corrected structures.
